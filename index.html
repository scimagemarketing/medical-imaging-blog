PACS in Medical Imaging: A Practical Guide

PACS stands for Picture Archiving and Communication System. It is used in healthcare settings to store, retrieve, manage, and distribute medical images digitally.

What Is PACS?

PACS helps healthcare professionals access medical images, such as X-rays, CT scans, MRI scans, and ultrasound studies. It can reduce reliance on physical film and support digital imaging workflows.

Key Features of PACS

  • Digital medical image storage and retrieval
  • Access to imaging studies from connected workstations
  • Support for sharing images across authorised departments
  • Integration with other healthcare information systems

Why Is PACS Important in Radiology?

PACS provides a central way to manage imaging studies. Its effectiveness depends on system configuration, security, network availability, and integration with clinical workflows.

Conclusion

PACS is an important part of digital medical imaging. Understanding its features can help healthcare organisations assess their imaging storage and access requirements.
